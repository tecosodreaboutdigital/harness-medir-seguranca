Step 4 of 5 · I

Inspect

How do we know it works? With sensors that produce evidence. "The operators like it" is an opinion.

What it is for

Measuring how the AI and the control room behave in a way anyone can verify in the log. A sentence like "false positives dropped from 180 to 60 per thousand alerts, and median time to triage stayed under 2 minutes" (illustrative numbers) is evidence.

The evaluator is not the executor: the AI does not vouch for its own accuracy. Verification comes from the operator, the recorded video, a second sensor or whoever went on site.

Silence is not safety

A dead camera that drops off the dashboard looks like a quiet night. Camera, network or server failures must show up as failures, never as "nothing happened". The saude-da-operacao skill shows how.

Questions

  1. Which sensors are on from day one, and where does each one get its data?
  2. Was the false-positive target per thousand alerts agreed before go-live?
  3. Is operator load (alerts per operator per hour) measured the way downtime is?
  4. Are operator disagreements logged, with a reason?
  5. Do camera, network or server failures show up as failures, or vanish as "nothing happened"?
  6. Who looks at these numbers, how often, and what happens when one crosses its limit?

What comes out

  • The chosen sensors, each with a definition, a source and a limit (see sensors).
  • A record of every alert, decision and action, with timestamps.
  • A periodic review on a fixed date, where the numbers are read by people who can change the harness.

In CCTV

  • Remote energy sites. Pilot criterion: critical alarm triaged within 5 minutes. The sensor is the time from the alert to the first logged decision, measured on every alert, not on a hand-picked sample.
  • Large events. Every staffing recommendation has its acceptance or rejection logged. The rejection rate, read together with the reasons, shows where recommendations are out of step with the command post.
  • Industrial site. Per-camera availability tells silence apart from blindness.
  • Retail. If alerts per operator per hour doubled after the new analytics went live, the AI gain turned into more volume. The project failed, even with high accuracy.

Anti-patterns

  • Measuring only the vendor's accuracy. A lab number says nothing about your scene, your lighting and your rain.
  • Sampling the good cases. Reviewing only alerts that became incidents hides the false positives and the whole operator cost.
  • Treating no alerts as success. Without an infrastructure sensor, a blind camera and a calm camera look the same.
  • An interface that does not allow disagreement. If the operator cannot record "the AI was wrong", the disagreement sensor does not exist, and the defect is in the design, not in the operator.
  • A number with no owner. An indicator nobody reads on a fixed date is decoration.
