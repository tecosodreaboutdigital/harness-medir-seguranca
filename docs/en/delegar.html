Step 3 of 5 · D

Delegate

How much can it do alone? Autonomy is set by the cost of being wrong, never by trust in the vendor or by the advertised accuracy.

What it is for

Declaring, for each use case, what the AI may do with nobody involved, what it only recommends, and what requires human confirmation and authorization.

Read it this way

A higher N means more human control, not more freedom for the machine. At N3 the AI still only flags; what grows is the number of people and records between the alert and the action.

Lock

If nobody can check the alert, there is nothing to delegate. The Lock overrides any level until a verification exists.

The full table is under control level.

Questions

  1. Which row of the control level table defines this case? Is it on record?
  2. Which actions may the AI carry out alone? Are they all internal, easy to undo and logged?
  3. Who confirms on video, who authorizes the action, and how fast does each need to respond?
  4. What happens if nobody responds in time? (the right answer is almost never "the AI acts alone")
  5. Does the interface let the operator disagree with the AI, and is the disagreement logged?
  6. Is there a way to check the alert? If not, the case is under Lock.

What comes out

  • The control level declared in the task contract, with the row that set it.
  • The list of actions by level: what is automatic, what is a recommendation, what requires authorization.
  • The roles that confirm and authorize, with deadlines and an escalation path.

In CCTV

CaseLevelWhy
Person in a restricted area of an industrial site: tag the footage and bring the camera up on the wallN1Internal, easy to undo, no third-party integration.
Failed or blocked camera in a residential building, with a maintenance ticketN1Internal and reversible. The ticket records the failure as a failure.
Queue longer than agreed at a large event, with a recommendation on where to reinforceN2Effects outside the control room. The command post accepts or rejects, and the log keeps the reason.
Vehicle with a suspicious plate at a bulk terminal gateN2 or N3N2 if the alert only creates an incident; N3 if it leads to blocking entry or stopping the driver.
Smoke outside the fence of a remote energy site, with team dispatchN3Dispatching a team has real cost and risk. The operator confirms on video first.
Watch-list match from face recognitionN3Approaching someone is hard to undo. The AI flags; the operator confirms and another person authorizes.

A cloned plate and a human face cannot share the level of a blocked camera: the same control room can run one case at N1 and another at N3 side by side.

Anti-patterns

  • Autonomy by vendor. "The system ships with automatic actions" decides nothing. The task contract does.
  • A deadline that turns into authorization. "If the operator does not answer in 2 minutes, the AI acts" turns the absence of a decision into a decision.
  • Confirming without looking. Human confirmation means watching the video, not clicking "OK" on a pop-up. Approval requests that come too often turn into reflex clicks.
  • Lowering control to save time. Starting at N1 a task that belongs at N3 "to speed up the pilot" is exactly the risk the level exists to prevent.
  • Delegating under Lock. If nobody can check the alert, build the verification first.
