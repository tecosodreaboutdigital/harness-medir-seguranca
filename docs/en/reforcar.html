Step 5 of 5 · R

Reinforce

What changes after a mistake? A one-off fix gets you through a shift. A change to the harness improves every shift after it.

What it is for

Turning every mistake into a permanent change in the environment: a rule, a threshold, a masked zone, a training session, one tool fewer. With a date and a version. This is what sets MEDIR apart from a generic improvement loop: you act on the environment, not on that night's answer.

Questions

  1. What exactly went wrong? In which step was the gap (map, equipment, delegation, inspection)?
  2. Which permanent change keeps the same mistake from coming back? A prompt rule, a threshold, a masked zone, a level change, training, a procedure?
  3. Who approves the change, and when does it take effect?
  4. Which sensor will show whether the change worked, and how soon?
  5. Was the change recorded with a date, a version and a reason?
  6. Did the mistake come back after an earlier change? Then the earlier change was not the right one.

What comes out

  • A "mistake that became a rule" entry, with date, version, what changed and why (format in CONTRIBUTING, in Portuguese).
  • The task contract and the prompt updated, with a new version number.
  • The date of the next harness review.

In CCTV

MistakePermanent change
Graffiti on a wall triggering intrusion alerts every nightMasked zone over the wall, with date and version; false positives on that camera tracked for two weeks.
Corroded railcar number misread, creating a cargo mismatchCross-check against a second record before opening an incident; low-confidence reads go to the operator, never straight into the client's system.
A plate read at 87% confidence treated as certainPrompt rule: below the agreed threshold, the answer always includes the alternative reading and asks for confirmation on video.
A dead camera read as an empty area at a large eventCamera failure raises its own infrastructure alert, and the area shows as "no view" on the command dashboard.
Operators ignoring alerts from one camera in the rainSeasonal threshold for that scene and a review of operator load in the rainy season; the reasons logged as disagreements backed the change.

Anti-patterns

  • A fix with no trace. Tweaking sensitivity "just for tonight" without recording it brings the same mistake back with nobody knowing why.
  • Blaming the operator. If the mistake got through because the interface did not allow disagreement, the fix belongs in the interface, not in training.
  • Swapping models as the default answer. A stronger model inside a harness with a gap just makes the failure more expensive.
  • Reinforcing without a sensor. A change with no number to follow is a bet.
  • Piling up rules without review. A rule that lost its reason takes up context, confuses the model and bloats the harness. Remove it at review time.

Any significant change can alter the map. At the scheduled review, go back to Map with what happened.
